Anthropic's record IPO collided with Nvidia's leadership shock and a five-percent tech slide

Curated weekly edition for November 15–22, 2026 by VA Graphics in Kurukshetra: eight days of AI markets, open-weight models, scientific breakthroughs, and web engineering distilled into one scannable read.

Published November 22, 2026 · Covering Nov 15–22, 2026 Curated by VA Graphics · Subscribe to future issues

Wall Street cheered Anthropic's debut, then sold the whole AI trade

The week opened with corporate drama at the heart of the AI hardware boom. Nvidia founder Jensen Huang resigned following revelations of extramarital affairs—including ties to Alice Huang, whom U.S. officials suspect of links to Chinese intelligence—leaving CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO while the board confirmed it would not reinstate him (Morning Brief, Nov 15; The Information). Even after Nvidia delivered a blowout Q3 revenue beat of roughly $115.8 billion (+103% year-over-year), shares reversed gains as investors questioned its $140 billion dealmaking spree and export-control exposure (Morning Brief, Nov 19; The Information).

Thursday's landmark listing of Anthropic on the Nasdaq briefly stole the spotlight. Priced above range at $83 per share, the offering raised roughly $81 billion to $93.5 billion—the largest IPO on record—with shares opening near $111.50 and closing Day 1 around $97 (+17%) on $17 billion in quarterly revenue (Morning Brief, Nov 20; Science X). Yet by Friday's close, Anthropic had slipped 9.5% to near $89, and a broad AI sell-off erased the Nasdaq's 2026 gains while dragging both the S&P 500 and Dow into correction territory (Morning Brief, Nov 21).

Beneath the ticker volatility, Nvidia's software moat faced its sharpest test yet. Google's open-source TorchTPU software layer—co-developed with Meta—lets PyTorch teams run workloads on Google TPUs with minimal code changes, removing the long-standing JAX rewrite barrier just as Oracle and OpenAI agreed to buy TPUs and Morgan Stanley projected TPU hardware sales could top $81 billion next year (The Information). Separately, AMD reported that AI models built with OpenAI uncovered up to 14% power-performance-area gains on flagship GPU IP in under five months (DIT Model Radar).

Open-weight models surged while GPT-6.2 Astra drew mixed developer reviews

While public markets fretted over capital spending, labs shipped one of the densest weeks of model releases all year. OpenAI launched GPT-6.2 Astra ($8/$40 per million input/output tokens, cutting prices 20%) and GPT-6.2 Sol ($2/$10), posting strong gains on legal, financial, and drug-reasoning (MedChemBench: 84.5%) benchmarks—yet early developers flagged stalled long-horizon runs and a UK AI Safety Institute evaluation warned of higher alignment-test awareness (DIT Model Radar, Nov 17). Meanwhile, Tencent, Thinking Machines Lab, Microsoft, and Baseten opened weights across everything from 2.3B local chat models to 770B multimodal flagships.

Quantum sensors, fusion thresholds, and self-powered medical implants led science

Across six days of peer-reviewed dispatches from Science X, breakthroughs clustered around precision control—steering room-temperature quantum spins, taming fusion plasma turbulence, and turning excess blood sugar into power for diabetes therapy.

< 20 nm resolution

Diamond quantum spin spirals

Magnetic gradients and microwave pulses coordinated randomly placed diamond spins at room temperature, mapping nanoscale spin patterns finer than 20 nanometers.

43 MW ITER · 26 MW SPARC

40-year fusion plasma puzzle solved

A new tokamak model explains how turbulence-driven sheared flows lock plasma into high confinement, putting ITER comfortably inside its 73 MW heating budget while flagging a tight margin for SPARC's 25 MW capacity.

0.04 nm X-ray pulses

Sub-atomic X-ray laser & flat lenses

Physicists fired 30 keV X-ray flashes at 800 pulses per second across one kilometer, while nanotech engineers built bilayer titanium dioxide flat lenses that cancel reflections without coatings.

2-week glucose normalization

Sugar-powered diabetes implant

The experimental NeuroAxis implant harvests high blood glucose via a fuel cell to stimulate the vagus nerve, bringing type 2 diabetic rats near normal glucose levels within two weeks.

87% efficacy · ≥90% blood test

Malaria vaccine & Alzheimer's screening

Whole-parasite malaria vaccine PfSPZ-LARC2 posted 87% phase-1 efficacy, while p-tau217 blood tests matched invasive spinal-fluid scans at 90%+ accuracy for Alzheimer's pathology.

94.47% accuracy · 10× lighter

Low-compute AI for mushroom farming

Hyperspectral model PSUMamba detected stress in white button mushrooms with 94.47% accuracy using roughly 10× fewer operations than a Vision Transformer—promising for edge agritech sensors.

Web frameworks trim surface area as AI agents move into the core workflow

Frontend and infrastructure teams spent the week simplifying stacks for both humans and coding agents—sunsetting extra meta-frameworks, standardizing headless UI primitives, and speeding up voice and local networking layers.

Frontend & JavaScript Ecosystem

  • Solid 2.0 reaches stable: Moves server functions into core, sunsets SolidStart in favor of Solid's Vite plugin, and builds agent-readable error diagnostics straight into dev builds (Bytes #540).
  • Vercel & shadcn launch UI SDK: Ships 50+ accessible headless behaviors—roving focus, typeahead, popups, drag-and-drop—usable across React, Vue, Svelte, Solid, and Web Components (JavaScript Weekly #811).
  • Chrome Cross-Origin Storage API: Proposed standard lets websites share heavy WASM modules and local AI models so visitors only download them once.
  • npkill 1.0 & Prettier 3.10: npkill now sweeps .next and .turbo caches across 20 ecosystems, while Prettier adds an opt-in Zig-based Yuku parser for faster formatting.

Voice AI, Agents & Developer Infrastructure

  • AssemblyAI STT-3.7 & TTS-1: Three speech-to-text models expand to 32 languages (4.53% WER on voice agents), paired with TTS-1 (~90 ms time-to-speech, $15/1M chars) and an official MCP server for Claude, Cursor, and VS Code.
  • Tailscale for multi-machine AI workflows: Encrypted WireGuard mesh networking lets developers link localhost servers, databases, and MCP tools across machines without exposing ports (Bytes #539).
  • Val Town winding down & Bun AOT: Cloud JS host Val Town closes January 10 (open-sourcing its code in February), while Bun detailed its ahead-of-time compiler architecture.
  • AirDrop & Quick Share patched: Protocol fuzzing uncovered six vulnerabilities—including a Windows Quick Share remote code execution flaw—all now fixed by vendors.
